## 2. What was actually wrong, and what fixed it

The investigation started from "SGLang's ITL p90 is ~27 % worse than ATOM's at
matched concurrency". Three findings, in order of size.

### (a) The ITL gap was an operating point, not a deficit

`--prefill-decode-interval 10 → 20` at c128 moved ITL p90 78.39 → 58.78 ms,
past ATOM's 61.5, at the cost of TTFT 8.50 → 13.22 s. Both pre-registered
criteria were tested as a pair: ITL passed, TTFT failed. So the two engines sit
at different points on **one prefill-versus-decode trade-off**, and SGLang's
decode is not intrinsically slower — it was being interrupted more often.

Corollaries, both measured and both settled: **TBO stays on** (off is worse on
ITL, TTFT and tok/s, and both engines' TBO is prefill-only by construction), and
**there is no reason to profile a decode step** — the pure-decode step is
*faster* at c192 than c160 because the batch is smaller.

### (b) The "knee at c160" never existed — hicache was off in every arm

c192 originally regressed 27 % on throughput with TTFT 59.71 s. The mechanism
was prefill-capacity saturation: prefill demand exceeded admission capacity, the
queue diverged, TTFT became queue wait, and throughput fell because the *decode
batch got smaller*, not because decode got slower.

Enabling the hicache CPU tier fixed it outright, single variable:

| | hicache off | hicache on | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| tok/s/GPU | 22,994 | 36,414 | **+58.36 %** |
| TTFT avg | 59.71 s | 9.11 s | **−84.74 %** |
| ITL p90 | 101.06 ms | 99.33 ms | flat |
| `#queue-req` p90 | 78 | **7** | queue gone |
| decode `#running-req` med | 15 | **23** | above c160's 20 |
| miss rate (windowed) | 8.15 % | **5.01 %** | −38.5 % |

36,414 beat the old c160 peak of 31,549, so **c160 was not a knee — it was the
ceiling of a machine with no L2 cache.** Every concurrency-curve number above
c128 measured before this is an artefact.

**Where the recovered hits come from is not where we predicted.** Only 1.4 pp of
the gain is the CPU tier serving reads directly; the larger 1.7 pp is the *GPU*
tier hitting more often, plausibly because a host backing store lets the device
pool evict without losing a prefix permanently. Do **not** repeat the older "a
third of all reuse comes from CPU" framing.

### (c) The OOR cliff: root cause was constexpr specialisation on context length

Arms were walking a cliff — `fp4-dptbo-c64-reclaim0` died with
`HSA_STATUS_ERROR_OUT_OF_RESOURCES` on a Triton **code-object load** (not a
tensor) at 0.00 GiB free, and `hicache-fp4-int20-c128` survived on luck with
free VRAM min 0.01 GB and 10 late loads.

Two upstream-shaped fixes and one accident, in decreasing order of what actually
mattered:

1. **`efaeb6f664` — pass `Wc` at runtime** so the prefill index kernels stop
   specialising on context length. This is the root cause: `Wc = page_idx.shape[1]`
   grew with context, and Triton loads one code object per constexpr combination.
2. **Shared-experts fusion frees ~10–14 GB** (weights 130.30 vs 133.75 GB/rank).
   This, not the preload, is what currently keeps arms off the cliff: free VRAM
   p10 went from 0.04 GB to 10.38–14.30 GB. Say it that way.
3. **`e485dc2436` — fp8 bounded logits buffer + kernel preload** (the old P0).
   Sound on its own terms, but **mis-scoped**: with the warning threshold raised
   to 1000 GB, the first complete measurement showed 503–516 late loads spread
   **engine-wide** — `alloc_extend_kernel` 152, `_prefill_cta_info_kernel` 65,
   `assign_req_to_token_pool` 56, and only 64 in the two kernels it targeted.
   Lazy Triton loading is an engine-wide property, not a DSV4 indexer one.

## 3. Rules established — do not re-litigate these with an arm

1. **TBO stays on.** Measured worse on all three axes with it off.
2. **Do not profile a decode step.** Decode is not slow; it was interrupted.
3. **Do not re-run the chunk-size diagnostic.** 16,384 → 8,192 gives ITL −25 %,
   TTFT +147 %.
4. **hicache only pays where GPU KV pool occupancy is high** — 96 % at c192,
   100 % at c256, **73 % at c128 = nothing** (CPU-tier hit 0.3–0.5 pp there).
   Do not add it to low-concurrency arms expecting anything.
5. **hicache and `--prefill-decode-interval` are orthogonal.** Interval 20's
   TTFT is *policy-imposed deferral* (`#queue-req` p90 = 9 at c128, 17 at c256 —
   trivial), not prefill demand, so cheaper prefill cannot pay it off. Combining
   them does not add.
6. **Shared-experts fusion is a memory result, not a throughput result.**
   +0.07 % tok/s at c128 is null; the ~10 GB of headroom is the point.
7. **ATOM's `run 33074134043` is unreachable** (ROCm blocks all classic PATs).
   It only ever decided whether to quote +27 % or +36 %.

## 4. Measurement rules that changed conclusions

- **Window the miss rate.** `Σnew/Σ(new+cached)` over a whole `server.log` is
  contaminated by a ~37 min cold-cache warmup contributing about half the
  `Prefill batch` lines. The original c192 pass criterion (`11.70 % → 8.6 %`)
  was **invalid** — both numbers were whole-log. Windowed reference: c96 5.59 %,
  c128 5.81 %, c160 5.96 %, c192 8.15 % → 5.01 % with hicache, c256 4.96–4.99 %.
- **Never report `gpu_cache_hit_rate` as "the cache hit rate"** on a hicache arm.
  At c256 the device tier read 0.766–0.822 while **overall was flat at 0.951** —
  hits demoted, they did not vanish. This printed a fake "cache collapse" once.
- **`ITL p90` is per-request TPOT**, p90 across requests — not the p90 of token
  gaps, so it cannot show a bursty tail.
- **`P90 intvty` is `1/p90(ITL)`**, not an independent metric. It is insensitive
  to queue wait: the broken c192 arm reads a healthy 9.9 alongside a 59.71 s
  TTFT. Always read it with TTFT.
- **`late loads = 0 (n/m)` means NOT MEASURED.** `triton_load_watch` is silent
  above 1 GiB free unless `SGLANG_TRITON_LOAD_WARNING_THRESHOLD_GB` is raised, so
  the 516 from the fusion arms and the 74 from an earlier arm are **not
  comparable**.
- **Replicate spread on this node is 5.67 %.** A smaller throughput delta is
  null — say "null", not "small improvement".
- **Cross-concurrency headline deltas are invalid**, because trace mix and ISL
  move with concurrency. `arm_report.py` flags them.

## 7. Node operating rules (all still live)

- **The node is shared.** Arm scripts wait for three consecutive idle checks and
  **refuse** rather than kill. A blind kill preamble destroyed another session's
  c96 arm on 09-02, and the giveaway was misread as "my own leftover processes".
- **The process check is not enough — use the VRAM gate too.** Twice the node
  showed 112–118 GB held evenly across all 8 GPUs with **zero visible
  processes** (a job in another container's PID namespace is invisible; its
  allocation is not). Starting then silently shrinks the KV pool and breaks the
  memory matching every comparison rests on. Both nodes' gates refuse, never
  kill.
- **`KV_OFFLOADING=hicache` is invalid.** Use `KV_OFFLOADING=dram` +
  `KV_OFFLOAD_BACKEND=hicache` + positive `TOTAL_CPU_DRAM_GB` + the undocumented
  `KV_OFFLOAD_BACKEND_METADATA='{"name":"hicache"}'`. Without the last one the
  arm exits 1 **after a fully successful benchmark** and writes no result JSON —
  re-run the aggregation alone, not the benchmark.
- **`--prefill-decode-interval` appears twice** in every resolved command: the
  launcher hardcodes 10, `EXTRA_ARGS` expands later, argparse keeps the last.
  Verify `'prefill_decode_interval'` in `server.log`'s `server_args`.
- **Keep `MEM_FRACTION_STATIC=0.90`.** ATOM runs `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.9`;
  0.85 breaks the matching and invalidates every arm on the board.
- **`/sgl-workspace` is on the container overlay and does not survive an image
  swap.** `/workspace` (ext4) and `/shared_nfs` (xfs) do.
- **Never `pkill -f` a pattern matching your own command line**, and **never
  edit a running bash script** (bash reads it by byte offset).
- **A dead server keeps answering `GET /metrics` with 200** and aiperf then waits
  forever. Verify an arm early, not at the end.

### One softening to §2(c) item 1

"`efaeb6f664` ... **This is the root cause**" is stronger than the evidence.
`Wc` was certainly *a* real unbounded specialisation axis, and closing it is
verified in isolation (sweep `Wc` 64…16384 → 0 loads). But the first complete
measurement — the same one §2(c) item 3 quotes — shows those two kernels are
only 64 of ~510 late loads and that they *still* load 4× per rank after the fix,
so a residual axis remains there too (likely Triton's automatic scalar
specialisation on the now-runtime `Wc`, e.g. the `Wc=1` ratio-0 path, or the MTP
draft backend's different `ring_stride`). Which kernel killed
`fp4-dptbo-c64-reclaim0` was never identified, so "root cause of the cliff" is
unproven. §2(c) item 2 is the load-bearing statement and it is correct: **fusion's
headroom, not the preload, is what currently keeps arms off the cliff.**
