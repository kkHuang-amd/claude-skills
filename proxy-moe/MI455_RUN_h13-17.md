Classification, with the decision rule used for each:
1. **Communication, high confidence.**
   - The aiter gfx1250 custom AR refuses TP8. Server log: "gfx1250 (MI450) custom allreduce only supports world_size <= 4, got world_size=8", so every AR runs the RCCL generic kernel.
   - Each AR costs at most 124 us at 64 KB. For comparison, MI355X custom AR is about 10-20 us.
   - Expected metric movement: per-call AR time and the comm share drop.
   - Alternative explanation tested: EP load imbalance.
     - c=2: per-rank MoE totals are within about 6% and per-rank AR totals are within ±4%. The 42% "wait" share is per-call arrival jitter, not systematic imbalance.
     - c=64: per-rank non-AR time rises monotonically from TP0 (21.6 ms) to TP7 (24.5 ms), and TP0 waits most. The cause of that gradient is unknown; it is not MoE alone.
2. **Memory-bound GEMV running on the wrong path, high confidence.**
   - `LagunaMoEGate` (`sglang/srt/models/laguna.py:129`) runs `F.linear` in fp32. The weight is [512, 16384] fp32, 32 MB per rank (replicated).
   - At 280 us per call that is about 120 GB/s, under 1% of HBM bandwidth.
   - Expected: with a bandwidth-bound fp32 GEMV, router time drops by more than 10x while fp32 numerics are kept.
3. **MoE (c=64):** 24%. It needs per-kernel roofline numbers before routing to the GEMM skill. Not routed yet.

### fp32 router GEMV: step 2, FlyDSL v1 (2026-10-23, h13-17)

`kernels/router_gemv_gfx1250.py` (FlyDSL 0.3.4.1; template: aiter `gemm_a8w8_splitk_reduce_gfx1250.py` and the conv1d buffer IO).
- Kernel: wave32, blocks of 8 independent waves. A wave owns EPW experts x one of KS K-chunks. Per step a lane does a 16 B fp32 weight load per expert and 4 bf16 x per token, with fp32 FMA. Then a wave `shuffle_xor` sum and a lane-0 store of `ws[ks,m,e]`; stage 2 is `torch.sum(ws, 0)` (fixed order). M is compiled in, at most 16 per launch.
- FlyDSL gotcha: `gpu.thread_id`/`block_id` are index-typed. Mixing them with `fx.Int32` arithmetic makes `fx.slice(..., (None, off))` SEGFAULT in `_infer_int_tuple_type`. Wrap them in `fx.Int32(...)` first.
- **Correctness vs a float64 reference** (logs `router_gemv_h13-17_1023/v1b_*.log`):
  - FlyDSL rel err is 1.1-1.5e-7 with top-16 100% at all M 1-64.
  - `F.linear` (hipBLASLt fp32) has rel err 2.2-2.4e-6 (about 20x worse) and top-16 96.9% at M=32. The earlier "mismatch" was the reference's error, not ours.
- **Speed** (us/call, idle clocks, 12 rotating weights; `F.linear` is 207-238 us at every M):

| M | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| epw2 ks8 | 9.3 | 13.2 | 16.1 | 31.7 | 144 | 282 | 544 |
| epw2 ks16 | 10.3 | 12.1 | 13.9 | 23.1 | 55.8 | 118 | 261 |
| epw2 ks64 | 11.0 | 13.2 | 14.4 | 20.0 | 31.6 | 82.1 | 206 |
| epw1 ks32 | 10.6 | 11.6 | 14.4 | 18.7 | 41.6 | 84.6 | 209 |

- Reading: M <= 4 is 14-24x faster than `F.linear` (best 9.3 us = 3.63 TB/s, vs the 5.47 TB/s read ceiling). Time grows super-linearly with M x EPW x steps: the fully unrolled K loop (`range_constexpr`) keeps loads live, so VGPR pressure / spills are suspected (not yet confirmed with an ISA resource dump). M > 16 re-reads W once per 16-token launch. At M=64 it is no better than `F.linear`.

### fp32 WMMA lane layout on gfx1250 (measured, `kernels/wmma_f32_layout_probe.py`)

`rocdl.wmma_f32_16x16x4_f32(vec8f32, a: vec2f32, b: vec2f32, c: vec8f32)` (raw MLIR `flydsl._mlir.dialects.rocdl`), wave32. One-hot probes give:
- A (16 rows x 4 k): lane l holds row `l % 16`; slot s holds `k = 2*(l//16) + s`.
- B (4 k x 16 cols): lane l holds col `l % 16`; slot s holds `k = 2*(l//16) + s`.
- C/D (16 x 16): element (m, n) is in lane `n + 16*(m//8)`, VGPR `m % 8`.
- A and B share the k assignment, so any physical-k permutation works if both use the same one.
- Gotcha: a 256-bit `BufferCopy` (8 x f32) fails in LLVM ("Do not know how to expand this operator's operand"). Buffer loads/stores top out at 128 bits; split them.
